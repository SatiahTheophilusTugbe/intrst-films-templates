# INTRST Films — Multi-Format Story Design Doctrine v1

Status: APPROVED DOCTRINE / implementation masters may remain WORKING until visual QA.

## Purpose
Define one coherent INTRST visual-and-story system across single-image editorial, narrative carousel, archive, evidence, and derivative formats. Formats inherit one publication identity but perform different narrative jobs.

## Shared visual grammar
- Image-dominant, premium editorial composition.
- Contemporary glossy photographic treatment; no automatic period/sepia/fake-archive look.
- Signal Blue `#3497FF`: INTRST identity, eyebrows, selected emphasis, precision markers.
- Dawn Gold `#C79A58`: emotional/editorial signature, tagline, rare micro-accent.
- Warm Ivory `#F1EEE6`: primary narrative reading layer.
- Midnight Navy `#0B1723`, Deep Slate `#182938`, Cinematic Black `#080A0C`: structural fields, scrims, contrast.
- Horizon Blue `#375A72`: quieter structural blue where Signal Blue would be too assertive.
- Cormorant Garamond: display/editorial moments.
- Manrope: brand, eyebrows, labels, CTA.
- Inter: metadata, source/date, utility copy.
- Authentic human/source material first. Never fabricate archival evidence, documents, interviews, letters, or real-person events.

## Shared story-retention engine
Every multi-beat asset should move through:
1. Hook — immediate tension/curiosity.
2. Promise — signals meaningful payoff.
3. Progression — each beat deepens the story.
4. Mini-hooks — each beat creates forward pressure without cheap suspense.
5. Pivot — fact, contradiction, or emotional reveal reframes the story.
6. Payoff — answer, consequence, or emotional release.
7. Residue — final meaning that lingers.

### Approved mini-hook methods
- contradiction
- delayed reveal
- reframe
- specificity
- escalation
- intimacy
- echo / callback

Mini-hooks may be textual or visual: crop changes, artifact reveal, evidence detail, quote, sudden quietness, source transition, or controlled color emphasis.

## Elegant storytelling guardrails
Do:
- reveal gradually;
- use specificity;
- trust the image;
- let one fact carry weight;
- use whitespace and silence;
- create a meaningful final beat.

Do not:
- over-explain every frame;
- make every line melodramatic;
- use clickbait phrasing;
- overuse accent colors;
- clutter evidence with arrows/circles/tabloid markup;
- end every asset with a marketing ask.

## Format roles
### Single-image editorial
Hero still. Usually full bleed. One resonant hook. No handle/CTA by default. Deep caption carries written depth.

### Narrative carousel
Swipeable editorial arc. Design follows story function, not one repeated card. Default 7-beat sequence: Tension → Scene → Context → Complication → Reveal → Consequence → Takeaway/Residue.

### Archive card
Artifact/source as protagonist. Clean provenance, no fake aging. Answers: what is this, why does it matter, what does it reveal?

### Evidence spread
Reasoning format. Shows claim → proof → meaning. Directs the eye from source to highlighted detail to implication without tabloid annotation.

## CTA / handle doctrine
No CTA/handle by default on single-image editorial, archive, evidence, quote cards, thumbnails, or carousel interiors.
CTA/handle may appear on carousel closing slide and Shorts/Reels end card. Tone must be publication-style, not aggressive engagement bait.

## Workstream boundary
WIN 5 owns visual grammar, format pacing logic, retention architecture, layout behavior, and design QA.
Editorial/Flagship owners retain claim-level wording, factual sequencing, thesis, source approval, and interpretation boundaries. WIN 5 must not silently rewrite upstream editorial doctrine.
