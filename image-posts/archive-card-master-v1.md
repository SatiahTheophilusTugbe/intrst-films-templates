# INTRST Films — Archive Card Master v1

Status: APPROVED FORMAT DOCTRINE / implementation template WORKING until rendered QA.

## Purpose
Present authentic archival material, documents, objects, headlines, books, maps, or source fragments with premium editorial restraint. Archive means authentic source material, not fake-old styling.

## Narrative job
An Archive Card should answer three questions:
1. What is this?
2. Why does it matter?
3. What does it reveal?

## Core structure
1. Artifact zone — the source/object itself.
2. Editorial framing — one short interpretive hook.
3. Provenance — source/date/origin.
4. Optional detail callout — crop, excerpt, or highlighted detail when it materially improves understanding.

## Visual modes
### Artifact as protagonist
Use when the source is visually strong.
- Do not force full bleed.
- Preserve meaningful borders/margins.
- Use dark or Warm Ivory structural field.
- Allow the artifact to breathe.

### Artifact + interpretation
Use when the artifact needs context.
- Primary source remains dominant.
- Add short explanatory copy and one detail inset or excerpt.
- Clearly distinguish original source from INTRST editorial annotation.

## Visual zones
1. Identity zone — `INTRST FILMS` or franchise label.
2. Artifact zone.
3. Archive label zone — `INTRST ARCHIVE`, `THE RECEIPTS`, or subject label.
4. Interpretive headline zone.
5. Provenance zone.
6. Optional detail-callout zone.

## Copy budgets
- Hook/headline: 4–12 words.
- Micro-caption: 12–35 words.
- Provenance: short and standardized.

## Color behavior
- Signal Blue: identity, archive label, precision marker.
- Dawn Gold: rare interpretive/emotional marker.
- Warm Ivory: main reading layer.
- Midnight Navy/Black or Ivory field chosen for source contrast.

## Source treatment rules
- Preserve authenticity.
- No fake aging, paper distress, VHS noise, film burns, fake typewriter overlays, or decorative archival damage.
- Never fabricate documents, handwriting, stamps, provenance, dates, or historical context.
- If an excerpt is highlighted, preserve enough surrounding source context to avoid misleading emphasis.
- Source/date labels must be legible but subordinate.

## Retention logic
Even a single archive card should contain a micro-arc:
- Hook: what are we looking at?
- Meaning: why this source matters.
- Residue: what the source reveals about the larger story.

## CTA rule
No CTA/handle by default. Archive authority takes precedence over marketing.

## QA
Reject if:
- artifact is decorative rather than meaningful;
- source and editorial annotation are visually ambiguous;
- fake-aging effects imply false provenance;
- copy explains more than the artifact earns;
- provenance is absent where source identity matters.
