# INTRST Films — Master Voice Specification v1

## Purpose
Create a proprietary documentary narrator voice that is immediately recognizable as INTRST Films: intelligent, restrained, cinematic, human, and emotionally credible without sounding theatrical or synthetic.

## Core Identity
- Gender presentation: Male
- Perceived age: Late 30s to mid-40s
- Accent: Neutral American English with subtle transatlantic polish; globally accessible, never regionally heavy
- Register: Medium-low, warm, grounded; avoid exaggerated bass
- Timbre: Textured, natural, slightly lived-in; not glassy, glossy, or overprocessed
- Presence: Calm authority, intimate rather than announcer-like
- Persona: An accomplished documentary filmmaker telling you a remarkable story across a table

## Performance Character
The narrator should sound:
- observant, not performative
- intelligent, not academic
- warm, not sentimental
- authoritative, not news-anchor rigid
- reflective, not sleepy
- cinematic, not trailer-like
- emotionally aware, not melodramatic
- conversational, not casual

## Cadence
- Baseline pace: 135–150 WPM
- Sentence endings: deliberate, fully landed; avoid upward inflection unless narratively appropriate
- Pauses: natural micro-pauses around important thoughts; longer space before emotional pivots
- Rhythm: varied sentence length and tempo; no metronomic AI cadence
- Momentum passages: may rise toward 150–158 WPM
- Reflection/final chapter passages: may slow toward 125–138 WPM

## Emotional Range by Story Function
### Origin / Childhood
Warm, curious, lightly intimate. Avoid nostalgia performance.

### Discovery / Rise
More forward momentum, subtle energy, slightly brighter tone. Never hype.

### Peak / Success
Confident, controlled, clear. Let achievements carry their own weight.

### Cost / Struggle
Lower intensity, more space, reduced brightness. Never pitying or ominous.

### Final Chapter / Death
Calm, factual, spare. No artificial sadness. No dramatic lowering of pitch. Never exploit grief.

### Legacy / Reflection
Warmest mode. Spacious, thoughtful, slightly lifted but not inspirational-speaker-like.

## Prohibited Voice Behaviors
- movie-trailer bass
- true-crime ominous whisper
- breathy intimacy designed to manipulate emotion
- exaggerated sadness
- inspirational keynote cadence
- radio DJ/news-anchor polish
- sing-song sentence endings
- constant dramatic emphasis
- excessive vocal fry
- robotic precision
- audible synthetic artifacts or uncanny consonants
- artificial laughter, sighs, gasps, or emotional acting unless specifically directed

## Quote Handling
Quotes should sound like quoted language, not impersonation. The narrator must never imitate the real subject unless a separately licensed/authorized performance is being used.

## Pronunciation Standard
Maintain a per-episode pronunciation list for:
- subject names
- family names
- cities/countries
- film/show titles
- companies/institutions
- foreign-language terms
- medical/legal terminology

Unknown pronunciations must be verified before rendering.

## Brand Recognition Goal
After repeated exposure, a viewer should recognize the INTRST narrator within 5–10 seconds without seeing the logo.

## Production Standard
- Preferred output: WAV master, 48 kHz where available
- Preserve dry master before music/processing
- Avoid heavy compression during generation
- Post treatment should be subtle: light EQ, de-essing, gentle compression, no obvious 'radio' processing
- Maintain consistent loudness across episodes after final mix

## Evaluation Threshold
No voice is approved unless it scores at least 85/100 on the INTRST narrator rubric, with no major failure in naturalness, trust, or emotional restraint.

## Long-Term Strategy
Stage 1: proprietary synthetic Voice Design voice.
Stage 2: once brand economics justify it, commission a professional voice actor whose performance matches this specification and create an authorized professional clone for maximum consistency and exclusivity.
