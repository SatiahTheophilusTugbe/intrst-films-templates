# INTRST Films — Long-Form Production System v1

## Purpose
Create premium documentary essays that feel authored, researched, cinematic, and human. AI is production infrastructure, not the visible aesthetic.

## Core Doctrine
- The death is the trigger.
- The life is the story.
- The legacy is the product.
- The audience is the asset.

## Editorial Objective
Transform a current event, anniversary, rediscovered figure, or evergreen subject into the definitive human story as quickly as editorial verification allows.

## Standard Runtime
Start with 8–15 minutes. Expand only when retention and story depth justify longer runtimes.

## Episode Architecture
1. **Cold Open (0:00–0:40)** — contradiction, unresolved question, 3–5 meaningful authentic visuals, restrained score.
2. **Before the World Knew Them (0:40–2:30)** — origins, place, family context, childhood, period documents and photography.
3. **The Break (2:30–5:00)** — the event or role that changed trajectory.
4. **Rise / Peak (5:00–8:00)** — achievements, public recognition, verified archival media.
5. **The Cost (8:00–10:30)** — pressure, setbacks, contradictions, publicly documented struggles; visually quieter.
6. **Reclaiming / Later Years (10:30–12:30)** — reinvention, later interviews, memoirs, returns, unfinished work.
7. **Final Chapter (12:30–14:00)** — concise verified facts only; no melodrama or speculation.
8. **What Remains (14:00–15:00)** — legacy, influence, closing thesis, reflective ending.

## Story Bible Inputs
Every episode must begin with:
- THE FACTS
- THE UNKNOWN
- THE THESIS
- THE CONTRADICTION
- THE EMOTIONAL QUESTION
- THE VISUAL MOTIF
- THE FINAL THOUGHT

## Anti-Hallucination Requirement
No final script is drafted directly from open-web research. Required sequence:
Sources → Claim Ledger → Life File → Story Thesis → Script → Fact Check → Final Edit.

Every factual sentence in the script must map to one or more approved Claim IDs. Interpretive sentences must be clearly defensible from approved claims and cannot introduce new factual assertions.

## Long-Form Visual Hierarchy
Priority order:
1. Verified authentic photography.
2. Licensed/public-domain/editorial archival footage.
3. Documents, headlines, letters, maps, timelines, archival objects.
4. Short transformative copyrighted excerpts when editorially necessary and defensible.
5. Motion graphics and typography.
6. Stock only when contextually accurate.
7. AI-generated imagery only for clearly non-documentary symbolic, environmental, or transition material.

## No-AI-Smell Rules
- No AI talking-head recreations of real people.
- No fake archival footage presented as real.
- No AI likenesses when authentic imagery exists.
- No endless generated cinematic B-roll.
- No generic AI voice cadence.
- No one music bed under the entire film.
- No template-heavy slide-show rhythm.
- No invented quotes, headlines, documents, locations, or artifacts.
- No fabricated private thoughts or motives.

## Documentary Evidence Grammar
Use evidence on screen, not decoration:
- newspaper clipping
- verified quote fragment
- interview transcript line
- yearbook/archive photo
- magazine cover
- handwritten note
- ticket/program
- map
- public record
- timeline
- location photography

Each artifact must have provenance stored in the Life File or Asset Ledger.

## Visual Pacing
Do not give every section equal visual weight. Alternate:
Impact → Context → Context → Impact → Quiet → Impact → Reflection → Quiet → Resolution.

Allow some sequences to be nearly photographic, some document-led, some typographic, and some clip-led. The brand should have a visual language, not a single repeated template.

## Copyrighted Film/TV Clip Policy
A copyrighted clip is never used because it merely looks good. It must serve commentary, criticism, analysis, or a specific biographical point.

Every clip must answer:
1. What claim, performance, decision, or cultural point does this clip illustrate?
2. Is the episode transforming the material through commentary or analysis?
3. Is only the amount reasonably necessary being used?
4. Could the viewer reasonably use this excerpt as a substitute for the original work?

If the answer to #1 or #2 is weak, reject the clip.

### Preferred Treatment
- brief excerpt
- narration immediately before, during, or after
- original soundtrack muted or minimized unless indispensable
- identify title/year when useful
- cut to photography, documents, headlines, interviews, or analysis quickly
- avoid uninterrupted entertainment sequences

## Rights Hierarchy
### Tier A — Preferred
- public domain
- licensed footage
- U.S. government material when applicable
- permission from rights holder/subject/estate
- Creative Commons used within exact license terms

### Tier B — Strong Editorial Sources
- properly licensed press footage
- EPK/trailer material where terms permit reuse
- interviews where usage rights are clear

### Tier C — Fair-Use Candidates
- movie/TV excerpts
- award-show excerpts
- unlicensed interview excerpts
- copyrighted news footage

Tier C requires explicit editorial justification and entry in the Clip Ledger. Legal review should be considered for higher-risk commercial releases.

## Narration Standard
Primary system: ElevenLabs or equivalent licensed synthetic narration.

Narration must feel directed, not generated:
- select one signature voice for a season/series where practical
- manually direct pacing, pauses, emphasis, and pronunciation
- vary sentence length
- retain silence after emotionally significant lines
- avoid wall-to-wall narration
- use room tone, archival sound, and natural pauses
- regenerate weak sentences rather than accepting first-pass delivery

Narration script should contain performance markup for pauses, emphasis, pronunciation, and intended emotional temperature.

## Music Standard
Preferred sources:
1. Original commissioned/generated tracks with commercially usable rights.
2. YouTube Audio Library / Studio music within its applicable terms.
3. Properly licensed production music.

Music should be scored by narrative function rather than one continuous bed:
- cold open tension
- origin curiosity
- ascent momentum
- cost/quiet restraint
- final chapter minimalism
- legacy resolution

Avoid generic inspirational swells and obvious AI-music repetition. Maintain cue sheets and license/provenance records for every track.

## Audio Mix Philosophy
Narration is primary. Music supports meaning and should frequently drop away. Archive audio can temporarily become the focus when historically meaningful. Avoid excessive loudness and constant sonic density.

## Final QC Gate
A long-form episode cannot publish until:
- all critical claims verified
- all quotes source-matched
- unknowns remain labeled unknown
- subject identity verified in every real-person asset
- copyrighted excerpts have editorial purpose documented
- music rights documented
- no generated media is presented as documentary evidence
- on-screen dates/names/titles checked
- captions/subtitles reviewed
- final script matches final picture edit
- platform copyright checks reviewed

## Premium Standard
Authentic footage establishes reality.
Narration establishes meaning.
Design establishes brand.
AI establishes efficiency.

The audience should experience a documentary studio, not an AI content pipeline.
