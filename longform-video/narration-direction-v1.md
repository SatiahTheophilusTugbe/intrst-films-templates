# INTRST Films — Narration Direction v1

## Purpose
Create a consistent, premium documentary narration performance that feels human, restrained, intelligent, and cinematic. AI voice is a rendering tool, not a stylistic gimmick.

## Core voice profile
- Calm authority; never announcer-like.
- Warm, observant, reflective.
- Intimate enough for human-interest storytelling, but never confessional unless the source material supports it.
- Avoid melodrama, trailer-voice delivery, exaggerated gravitas, artificial breathiness, and uniform emotional intensity.
- The narrator should sound like a thoughtful documentary producer guiding the audience through evidence, not selling a story.

## Baseline pace
- Default: 142–154 words per minute.
- Reflective passages: 128–140 WPM.
- Momentum / rise sequences: 155–166 WPM maximum.
- Final chapter: 132–144 WPM.
- Never accelerate simply to fit runtime; edit the script instead.

## Performance scale
Use emotional temperature 1–5 internally.

1 — Neutral factual: dates, locations, credits, chronology.
2 — Curious / observational: origin, discovery, context.
3 — Engaged / energized: breakthrough, ascent, public recognition.
4 — Reflective / emotionally weighted: grief, cost, contradiction, private struggle.
5 — Reserved for rare climax only. Never theatrical. Maximum one or two short passages per episode.

## Pause system
- `/` = micro-pause (~0.15–0.25 sec)
- `//` = sentence-level reflective pause (~0.4–0.7 sec)
- `///` = chapter or emotional transition (~0.9–1.4 sec)
- `[hold]` = allow image, archival audio, or quote to breathe for 1.5–3 sec; normally no narration.

Do not over-mark pauses. Natural punctuation remains primary.

## Emphasis system
Use emphasis sparingly. Mark only the one phrase that carries the sentence's meaning.

Example:
She was not discovering fame. Fame had already become **normal**.

Avoid emphasizing names, dates, and every dramatic noun.

## Sentence design
- Prefer 8–22 word sentences.
- Mix short impact lines with longer explanatory lines.
- Avoid three consecutive sentences with the same rhythm.
- Avoid artificial rhetorical triplets unless editorially earned.
- Do not use empty phrases such as “little did she know,” “what happened next,” or “her life would never be the same.”

## Quotes
- Narrator introduces the speaker and context.
- Direct quotes must match verified source language and copyright limits.
- Quotes may be voiced by the main narrator unless a licensed archival clip exists.
- Do not imitate the real subject’s voice.
- Shift delivery slightly: slower, less interpretive, clean attribution.

## Names and pronunciation
Each episode must include a pronunciation ledger:
- person / place / title
- phonetic rendering
- source of pronunciation if uncertain
- approved spelling

Never guess uncommon pronunciations when authoritative audio can be checked.

## Chapter performance
### Cold Open
Temperature 3–4. Controlled tension. Begin close and conversational, not loud.

### Origin / Before Memory
Temperature 2. Curious, spacious, intimate.

### Rise / Breakthrough
Temperature 3. Slightly more pace and forward motion.

### Peak
Temperature 3. Confidence without celebration-for-its-own-sake.

### Cost / Contradiction
Temperature 3–4. Slow slightly. Do not sound judgmental or mournful.

### Grief / Personal Struggle
Temperature 4, but restrained. No sadness performance. Let facts and silences carry weight.

### Final Chapter
Temperature 2–3. Plain, careful, exact. Death facts are never performed for drama.

### Legacy / What Remains
Temperature 2–3. Reflective, open, intellectually satisfying. Avoid eulogy clichés.

## Death and sensitive material
- State verified facts plainly.
- If cause is unknown, say that once and move on.
- No ominous vocal shift for unconfirmed circumstances.
- Never imply motive, diagnosis, cause, or intent not present in the Claim Ledger.
- Avoid sensational phrases: “tragic end,” “shocking death,” “demons,” “spiraled,” unless the exact framing is editorially justified and approved.

## Audio engineering target
For production handoff:
- Render clean narration without music baked in.
- Prefer WAV, 48 kHz, 24-bit when supported; high-quality MP3 only for drafts.
- Maintain consistent voice model, settings, and seed/consistency options where provider supports them.
- Generate chapter-by-chapter rather than one entire episode when practical, preserving settings across renders.
- Keep raw and mastered files separately.

## ElevenLabs workflow
1. Final script is approved against Claim Ledger.
2. Remove internal Claim IDs from spoken copy but keep them in production script.
3. Apply narration markup and pronunciation ledger.
4. Render a short audition passage with 2–3 candidate voices.
5. Score voices using the INTRST voice rubric.
6. Lock one primary narrator and one backup.
7. Render by chapter.
8. Human listen-through before edit.

## Voice audition rubric — 100 points
- Naturalness / absence of synthetic cadence — 25
- Credibility / documentary authority — 20
- Emotional restraint — 15
- Warmth / human connection — 15
- Long-form listenability — 15
- Pronunciation accuracy — 5
- Brand distinctiveness — 5

Minimum approval: 82/100.

## Hard rejects
Reject a voice if it consistently exhibits:
- trailer-announcer cadence
- dramatic downward inflection every sentence
- breathy pseudo-intimacy
- robotic clause timing
- excessive enthusiasm
- monotone across emotional chapters
- conspicuous AI pronunciation errors
- celebrity imitation

## Production principle
The listener should remember the story, not the voice technology.
