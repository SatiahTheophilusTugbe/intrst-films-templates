# INTRST Films — Sonic Bible v1

## Objective
Build a recognizable sonic identity without turning every film into the same soundtrack. Music supports story architecture, pacing, and emotional contrast; it must never manipulate beyond the evidence.

## Sources
Preferred music sources:
1. Original compositions generated or commissioned for INTRST.
2. YouTube Studio / Audio Library tracks with usage terms logged.
3. Licensed production music where appropriate.

Never assume AI-generated music is automatically free of rights issues; store provider, plan/license status, generation date, track ID, and usage terms in the cue ledger.

## Mix doctrine
- Narration is always intelligible first.
- Silence is an active creative tool.
- Do not run one continuous music bed underneath the full documentary.
- Let archival audio breathe when it contains meaning.
- Avoid obvious “sad piano = death” scoring.
- Avoid blockbuster trailer percussion for ordinary biography beats.
- Avoid generic corporate inspiration cues.

## Seven recurring score families

### 1. ORIGIN — “First Light”
Use: childhood, hometown, family context, earliest ambition.
Character: intimate, curious, lightly textured.
Palette: felt piano, soft acoustic detail, restrained strings, room tone, subtle analog texture.
Tempo: 55–82 BPM.
Avoid: sentimentality, music-box child clichés.

### 2. DISCOVERY — “The Door Opens”
Use: first break, opportunity, recognition, movement into a larger world.
Character: forward-looking, anticipatory.
Palette: light pulse, piano motif, restrained percussion, warm synth bed.
Tempo: 80–105 BPM.
Avoid: triumphant advertising music.

### 3. MOMENTUM — “In Motion”
Use: career rise, montage, awards, projects, cultural visibility.
Character: elegant momentum, not hype.
Palette: rhythmic strings, subtle drums, bass pulse, textural synth.
Tempo: 100–124 BPM.
Avoid: EDM drops, sports-promo aggression unless subject genuinely calls for it.

### 4. COST — “Under the Surface”
Use: contradiction, pressure, identity conflict, private consequences.
Character: uneasy but human.
Palette: sparse low piano, distant textures, restrained cello/viola, irregular pulse.
Tempo: 48–78 BPM.
Avoid: horror drones, true-crime menace, ominous sub-bass for unproven claims.

### 5. TENSION — “Unresolved”
Use: factual uncertainty, pivotal decisions, public controversy, high-stakes transitions.
Character: disciplined tension.
Palette: pulse, muted percussion, subtle ticking texture, low strings.
Tempo: 65–100 BPM.
Avoid: implying guilt, danger, or causation through music when facts do not support it.

### 6. FINAL CHAPTER — “Stillness”
Use: verified final years, death, aftermath.
Character: sparse, exact, dignified.
Palette: minimal piano/strings, ambient room, occasional sustained tones.
Tempo: 45–68 BPM or non-metric.
Rule: frequently begin with silence. Music may enter after facts are stated rather than scoring the death announcement itself.
Avoid: funeral clichés, swelling grief, manipulative crescendos.

### 7. LEGACY — “Afterimage”
Use: influence, what remains, reflection, closing thesis.
Character: expansive but unresolved enough to feel human.
Palette: recurring motif from Origin transformed into fuller arrangement when possible.
Tempo: 62–92 BPM.
Avoid: forced triumph or inspirational-commercial endings.

## Signature strategy
Rather than one permanent theme song, use a recurring 3–5 note INTRST motif across selected original cues. The motif may appear in different instrumentation and tempo across Origin, Cost, and Legacy. It should be subtle enough that the audience feels continuity before consciously recognizing it.

## Episode scoring architecture
Typical 12–15 minute film:
- 0:00–0:15: silence / archival sound / minimal motif
- Cold open: Tension or Cost, lightly
- Origin: First Light
- Break: The Door Opens
- Rise: In Motion
- Cost: Under the Surface
- Final chapter: silence → Stillness
- Legacy: Afterimage, ideally resolving or reinterpreting the Origin motif

Do not force every family into every episode.

## Music density target
Approximate guideline:
- 55–70% of runtime may contain some music in highly visual essays.
- 30–45% should contain silence, room tone, archival sound, interview audio, or unscored narration.
- Sensitive passages should often reduce instrumentation rather than increase it.

## Cue transitions
Preferred:
- editorial hard cut on chapter shift
- filtered tail into archival audio
- motif handoff between cues
- short silence before emotionally important line

Avoid constant crossfades that make the soundtrack feel like an automated playlist.

## Loudness and ducking
- Narration always dominates.
- Music should generally sit 12–20 dB below narration depending on density.
- Automate music around key phrases instead of static ducking throughout.
- Archival speech gets the same priority as narration when editorially central.

## Cue provenance
Every track must be represented in the music cue ledger with:
- cue_id
- track_title
- score_family
- source/provider
- creator/model
- generation or acquisition date
- license/usage basis
- file URL/path
- episode time-in/time-out
- duration
- notes

## Rights rule
No track enters the final master unless its usage basis is known and stored.

## Production principle
Sound should make INTRST feel authored and cinematic, never emotionally coercive or mechanically generated.
